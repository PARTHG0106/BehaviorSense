## What it reports and why both numbers

Top-1 **and** mean per-class, always. On an 88× imbalance they answer different questions — top-1 is dominated by `Walk`'s 3,942 clips, mean per-class is what the Toyota paper reports precisely so the tail can't be ignored. Publishing one would be a silent choice about which classes matter.

The checkpoint is selected on **mean per-class**, not top-1, for the same reason: top-1 would pick the epoch that best fits the majority class.

Class weighting is effective-number (Cui et al.), not inverse frequency — 1/n would weight `Cutbread`'s 45 clips 88× a `Walk` clip, which assumes each tail sample carries 88× the information. Tail clips of one action by one subject overlap heavily; they don't.

Every per-class accuracy prints its **test support and its own granularity**. `Cutbread` will have roughly 15 test clips, so its accuracy moves in steps of ~7 points — that belongs beside the value, not discovered when someone quotes it.

```python
IN = pathlib.Path("/kaggle/input")
S  = next(IN.rglob("scripts/train_adl.py"))
SH = next(p for p in IN.rglob("toyota_*_20hz_*.npz")).parent
print("shards dir:", SH)
r = subprocess.run([sys.executable, str(S), "--shards", f"{SH}/*.npz",
                    "--n-classes", "22", "--stream", "bone", "--epochs", "40",
                    "--out", "/kaggle/working/runs/adl_toyota_bone"],
                   capture_output=True, text=True,
                   env={**os.environ, "PYTHONPATH": str(S.parents[1] / "src")})
print(r.stdout[-9000:] or r.stderr[-4000:])
```

**Then:** Save Version → publish as `behaviorsense-toyota-adl-runs`.

`--n-classes 22` is mandatory — without it the run refuses within seconds instead of burning the session.
