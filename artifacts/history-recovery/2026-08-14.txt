## The transition-prior hypothesis was wrong

| decoding | top-1 | mean-class |
|---|---|---|
| per-window argmax | 0.385 | 0.152 |
| Viterbi, hand-set prior (0.90) | 0.396 | 0.112 |
| Viterbi, **fitted** prior (mean 0.764) | **0.398** | 0.116 |
| argmax + logit-adjust τ=0.25 | 0.319 | **0.202** |
| Viterbi fitted + logit-adjust τ=0.25 | 0.374 | 0.174 |

I said the 0.90 self-transition was too sticky for a 1 s stride and that fitting it from 165,109 training windows would recover the lost mean-class. The fitted value **is** 0.764, so the prior was too sticky — and correcting it recovered **0.004 of the 0.040**. Effectively nothing.

The regression isn't a mis-specified rate. It's intrinsic to max-product decoding over a posterior whose head class holds 39% of the mass: whatever the transition probabilities, the cheapest path through a short rare-class run is to absorb it into the surrounding majority. I've recorded that in `results/evaluation.md` as a failed prediction rather than quietly dropping it.

**What did work is ordering: adjust first, then smooth.** 0.112 → **0.174**, recovering 0.062 of the 0.090 gap while keeping most of the top-1 benefit (0.374 vs 0.319 unsmoothed). Raising the tail classes means smoothing can no longer erase them cheaply.

## One more thing before you act on this

Plain argmax + adjustment wins on label quality at 0.202. Read alone that says "drop smoothing" — and acting on it would be a mistake, because Agent 3 derives `walking_bouts` and `mean_bout_duration_s` from segment **counts**, not labels. A decoding that shatters one true walking stretch into nine reports nine bouts and a ninth of the mean duration while scoring identically per window.

So I added `fragmentation()` — predicted segments over true segments, 1.00 ideal — as a column beside every row. That's the number smoothing actually exists to protect, and until it's on the table the argmax-versus-Viterbi choice can't be made honestly. Verified on hand-built cases: 1.00 perfect, 8.3× flickering, 0.7× over-merged.

**P14** also pins the fitted matrix as row-stochastic, checks it recovers a higher self-transition from deliberately sticky sequences, and asserts the emergency floor into `falling` survives at exactly 0.05 — a counted matrix leaves a 0.5%-prevalence state unreachable, and a naive floor-then-normalise shaves it back below itself.
