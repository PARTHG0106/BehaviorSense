Yes — one thing upstream genuinely required fixing, and it's the most consequential defect found in this project so far.

## P1 was never person-disjoint

The shards store **video ids** as `subjects`. That decision rests on a claim recorded in `docs/07_kaggle_plan.md`: *"Charades subject ids do not exist publicly."*

**That claim is false.** [`Charades_v1_train.csv` has carried a `subject` column all along](https://prior.allenai.org/projects/data/charades/README.txt) — a unique id for each of the **267 actors**, ~30 videos each. At that ratio, a video-id split puts essentially every *person* on both sides. Same actor, same home, same mannerisms in train and val. The disjointness assertion could never catch it, because the ids really are disjoint — it's the people behind them that aren't.

The sharpest irony: `subject_from_path()` was built to prevent *exactly this* in the fall corpora ("the ids are disjoint — the PEOPLE behind them are not," GMDCSA's `Subject 1/01.mp4` vs `Subject 2/01.mp4`). We committed the same failure on the biggest corpus, and the CSV that fixes it is already inside `charades-480p`, attached to every session.

**What this means for the numbers:** every P1 figure to date is video-disjoint, not person-disjoint, and likely optimistic. The fall head is less affected (its corpora already use real people, plus ADL negatives), but the ADL mean-class/macro-F1 numbers need the caveat — now written into `results/evaluation.md` and the README limitations, ahead of anyone else spotting it.

**The fix, all in place and tested (166/166, full suite including training):**
- `load_subject_map()` / `remap_subjects()` — video→actor remap, applied at *split time only*; shards keep video ids, which sequence reconstruction still needs. Non-Charades ids pass through, coverage is reported, <50% coverage refuses to train.
- `train_adl.py --subject-map`, notebook 03 passes it automatically when the CSV is present, notebook 04 auto-detects it and prints the split mode next to P1.
- **Resume guard extended**: a checkpoint trained under one split identity is refused under the other — resuming across identities would smuggle the old boundary's leakage into the new protocol. (Also added `sampler`/`tau_train` to that guard while there.)
- **S13** proves it: on a Charades-shaped fixture, the video-id split leaks 4 of 5 actors onto both sides; the actor-id split leaks zero. Positive control included, so the assertion can't pass vacuously.

Slot tracking (S8), the best-covering window labelling, fall labelling by true frame position, `normalise()` dropout handling, bone flip equivariance, the MMDetection-free preflight — all already regression-tested against the failures they once had.
