## The television

Both your screenshots have a TV with people on it, and RTMO detects them. A screen in the corner never shares a frame with the room — which makes it look *exactly* like a person who stepped out of detection, so disjoint spans alone would fold it into the resident and attribute a broadcast's motion to her.

The guard is arithmetic, not recognition — and that matters, because `tv_duration_s` and `drinking_events` need RT-DETR, but this doesn't:

> Could one walking person have got from the previous fragment's last box to this one's first box in the gap? At ~1.2 body-heights/second, with one body-height of slack for tracker handoff.

Body-heights because a bounding box's height tracks how tall the person looks, so it needs no camera calibration and no pixels-per-metre guess. In the test: the resident at the counter is 14 body-heights from the TV person with a 4 s gap — unreachable, rejected. The same person re-detected nearby merges, and a walk across the kitchen over 6 s stays reachable.

The slack is deliberately generous, and the reason is asymmetric. Wrongly *splitting* one person re-creates the discarded-activity bug you just had me fix; wrongly *merging* a TV person adds spurious activity that the declared caveat at least covers. So the guard errs toward merging.

This required replacing interval scheduling with longest-path over a DAG — the reachability constraint isn't transitive, so A→B and B→C doesn't imply A→C, and the best chain ending at *i* must consider every compatible predecessor. With single-digit track counts the quadratic scan is free. `track_boxes` now crosses the wire alongside `track_spans`.

I should be straight about the limit: this rejects a TV person by *distance*, not by knowing what a television is. A screen close to the resident would still get through. The real fix is RT-DETR detecting the TV and dropping person boxes inside it — the same work `tv_duration_s` needs, so both land together. The `results/evaluation.md` claim that Toyota was "not yet trained on" is stale too — the served checkpoint *is* the Toyota one. I've noted both.
