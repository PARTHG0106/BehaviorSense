## Qwen3.5-9B: better model, wrong fit

It's real — released February 2026, Apache-2.0, 9B dense, hybrid Gated DeltaNet + full attention, 262K context, and the reported benchmarks are genuinely strong for the size. Four problems, in order of severity:

**It doesn't fit the serving GPU.** 9B at 16-bit is ~18 GB of weights before any KV cache. A T4 gives ~15 GB usable, P100 gives 16. It only runs sharded across T4×2, and cross-device generation over PCIe is slow. Quantising to fit is the thing we explicitly refused — it would trade the numbers in `results/evaluation.md` for ones nobody has measured.

**The DeltaNet path needs `causal_conv1d` and `fla`, and without them [transformers' own docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/qwen3_5) say it "silently falls back to slower and more memory-hungry" paths.** Silent, memory-hungry, on a 16 GB card, is the precise failure class this project keeps paying for. Both are CUDA extensions that must match the Kaggle image, and notebook 04 is offline — so they'd have to be prebuilt wheels staged into `behaviorsense-wheels`.

**It wants `transformers >= 5.2`** ([NVIDIA's docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/megatron-bridge/nightly/models/qwen/qwen35-vl.html)) — a major version bump under `outlines>=1.0`, which is what provides the grammar-constrained decoding. That's an integration risk with a one-month deadline and a 2-hour feedback loop.

**All Qwen3.5 variants are natively multimodal.** That weakens your own architectural claim from structural to procedural: today "the LLM never sees pixels" is true because the model *cannot*; with a VLM it's true because *we don't send them*. The API surface still enforces it — no endpoint accepts images — but a text-only Agent 4 is the version an examiner can't poke a hole in.

**And the gain is on the wrong axis.** Hallucination sits at ~8%, stable across four reproduced runs, and grammar constraints didn't move it (p=0.79). The verifier catches unfaithful claims regardless of who wrote them — that's the contribution, and it's model-agnostic. What a stronger model buys is *yield*: fewer withheld claims, fewer truncations, fewer schema rejections. Real, but secondary.

### What I'd do instead

1. **Keep Qwen2.5-7B as the measured baseline.** Four reproduced runs a month from a deadline is a result, not a placeholder.
2. **If you want the upgrade, try Qwen3.5-4B, not 9B.** ~8 GB at 16-bit — fits a single T4 with headroom, same architecture family, and the reported small-model results are the strongest part of that release. Same harness, cheap ablation.
3. **For the live demo, route Agent 4 through the AgentRouter API.** Notebook 05 has internet ON. That frees all 16 GB for perception and activity, removes the model-size ceiling entirely, and the verifier is exactly what makes an untrusted remote author safe — C1–C4 check arithmetic regardless of who wrote the sentence. Live claims already go through `escapeHtml` before they reach the DOM.

   Keep local Qwen for notebook 04's numbers though. A hosted model changes under you, and a hallucination rate measured against a moving target isn't reproducible.

Three models behind one verifier is a better dissertation result than one bigger model — it demonstrates the guarantee is a property of the architecture, not of the checkpoint.

**Reporter** (`src/behaviorsense/agents/reasoning/reporter.py`)

- **Sampling → greedy** for both constrained and free decoding arms. The defect: free arm inherited `do_sample=True, temperature=0.7` from `generation_config`; constrained arm only passed `max_new_tokens` to `outlines`. Two arms decoded differently, so the difference measured temperature, not grammar. `SAMPLING_FLAGS` strips both; `force_greedy()` applied before every run
- **`lying_duration_s` replaces `sleep_proxy_duration_s`** — the latter never existed on `DailyFeatures`, so the model was never offered a sleep-related feature. A dead entry reads as covered

- **Bf16 capability warning** at load: T4/P100 have no native bfloat16 (sm_75/sm_60); bf16 is emulated and slow. Warns rather than silently switching to fp16 (numerics change)
- **`max_memory` bounds** for `device="auto"`: the perception child process opens its own CUDA context (~2 GB) on the same cards; without a bound `auto` fills both and leaves the child nothing
- **Logit sanity check** at load: one forward pass, checked for NaN/inf; catches fp16 overflow on bf16-trained weights
